Both start at a shared visible floor (22% of the chart) and diverge — the grey one climbs to 78% over 1700ms, the purple answers it at 34% (950ms, starting at 1s). THE NUMBERS ARE REAL, AND ONLY ONE IS QUANTIFIED. 39% is the top of our cited 25–39% range. A “91% with us vs 45% without us” chart would be invented, would fail the content-integrity tests, and is the kind of claim that draws 2.3.1 scrutiny on a health app. Pep uses the name the user chose (“Sushi” here) — from useCompanionName, so it reads as their companion, not a stock mascot.

The line traces itself over 2200ms on the same both-ends bezier as the lean-mass bars, and the shaded fill is revealed by a clip that follows where the STROKE actually is — sweeping it linearly puts the shading 22px ahead of its own outline at the crest, because the steep rise burns path length without moving in x. Skipped when they pick “None yet” or select nothing: a nausea curve is a strange thing to show someone reporting no symptoms, and it reads as being told what is coming rather than being heard. THERE IS DELIBERATELY NO SECOND CURVE. DreamMe’s version draws a high “Without tracking” line against a low “DreamMe” line, which asserts that using their app roughly halves side-effect intensity. No such data exists — not for them and not for us — and it is the same invented-comparison claim we refused on the lean-mass bars. Drawing it would fail the content-integrity tests and is exactly the kind of efficacy claim that draws 2.3.1 review on a health app. What replaces it: one curve that is actually true (GI effects are dose-dependent and attenuate — well documented), with the honest differentiator carried by the copy rather than by a fake line. We do not claim to lower the curve; we claim to tell you where on it you are standing, which is a promise the product actually keeps via symptom-to-shot linking. Arguably the stronger pitch: it teaches the user something real in the moment they are most anxious. Personalisation: the card title names THEIR worst-ranked pick (“Nausea after a dose change”), and the x-axis anchors to their shot day when the schedule is weekly.
