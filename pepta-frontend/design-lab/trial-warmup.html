Pre-paywall trial warm-up

Two screens inserted between reveal (32) and the paywall (which moves 33 → 35), plus a paywall redesign. The job: shift the frame from “I'm being asked for money” to “I'm being given something” before any price is visible. Mechanics adapted from DreamMe — gift framing, a micro-commitment ladder (two chosen taps before the wall), deferred price disclosure, value demonstration right before the ask, and radical transparency about the charge. Their palette stays theirs; everything below is built from Pepta's shipped components. Funnel context: 9/9 of users who reach screen 6 reach screen 32 — length is not what loses this audience.

Rationale & handoff notes

Carousel picks. Dose countdown → level curve → progress-with-muscle → side-effect patterns. Order is deliberate: logistics first (the immediate anxiety), the differentiator second (the curve nothing else has), outcome third, care last. Captions are outcomes in the user's words, never feature names.

Badge conflict — resolved by scope, not by stacking. One badge per row stays the rule (the shipped PlanCard has one slot; two floats would collide at top-right and read as noise). Yearly's slot always carries SAVE 67%. The trial is communicated by the timeline component whenever the selected plan carries it; the green 3 DAYS FREE badge on monthly appears only in the monthly-only state, where the trial genuinely differentiates the rows. If the trial ever lands on both plans, that badge disappears entirely (C1). Green (fiber #34C759) keeps “free” visually distinct from “selected” (primary).

New mascot assets required (2). Pep · gift — both arms raised holding the #E25CC4 heart; built as overlays on the existing body per Mascot.tsx's own convention (mirrored wave-arms + one heart path + happier eye position). Pep · peek — top-of-barrel crop, eyes over an edge; a clip of the existing body, no new drawing. Both stay react-native-svg overlays until the 3D renders are commissioned.

What had to bend. (1) The design-lab's dark paywall “wall” is retired — the shipped paywall is light, and A/B must flow seamlessly into C, so C stays on the app ground with the convo progress bar ending at A/B. (2) The timeline is a new component with no existing equivalent; it borrows the level-card's surface, radius-16 icon squares (#F1EDFF), and hairline rules, so it introduces zero new colors or radii. (3) Plan cards go side-by-side (from stacked) to give the timeline vertical room — same card anatomy, price scale drops 20→19. (4) Screen A intentionally breaks the funnel's type rhythm with a centered layout — it is the only centered screen in onboarding, which is what makes it read as a moment.

Open decision, informed not resolved. The warm-up promises “3 days on us” twice before the wall. In C2 (today's monthly-only reality) a yearly-preselected wall keeps that promise only if the user spots the monthly badge — a real broken-promise risk after two screens of gift framing. C1 is the design the sequence wants: either add the intro offer to the annual product, or preselect monthly in the treatment arm. Both change what the experiment measures, so it's a product call — but shipping A+B with C2-yearly-preselected is the weakest of the four combinations and the data (2 taps / 1 trial / 0 purchases from 8) will stay noisy at this volume.

Numbers. Brief specifies $39.99/year; the shipped fallback copy renders $40.00/yr (FALLBACK_PRICING in paywallPricing.ts). Real store products drive the display either way, but the fallback should be aligned to $39.99 at implementation time. Trial length, day labels, and the charge date are all data-driven from the loaded intro offer — the Apple-side offer expiry can change the trial without touching these screens.
